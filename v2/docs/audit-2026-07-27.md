# Gridiron GM v2 — franchise systems audit
**27 July 2026.** Based on reading all 13.4k LOC of `v2/` plus four headless experiments:
a 20-season franchise run (seed 12345), a 15-season cap/aging probe (seed 999), a
3-seed × 12-season parity probe, and a full-season box-score instrumentation pass.

---

## Short answers to your questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Do stats reset each season? | **No — and that's correct.** `Player.stats` is an array of per-season lines, appended forever, never cleared. Career totals derive from it. |
| Do historical records get saved? | **Partly.** Single-game records are captured live into `state.records` (top 5 per category, kept forever). Season/career records derive from `p.stats`. Final standings *are* written to `state.history[].standings`… **and then never read by anything.** After the rollover `state.games = []`, so `/standings`, `/stats`, `/schedule` and the hub all render 0-0 for every past season. There is no way in the game to look up who went 14-3 in 2029. |
| Does progression/regression work? | It runs, but **it's directionally broken.** League average OVR inflates 63.6 → 68.5 over 20 seasons, the 85+ population triples (22 → 69), and average OVR *rises monotonically with age*: 22 → 64.6, 26 → 67.4, 30 → 69.4, 34 → 71.8. Old players are the best players in the league. |
| Does age hurt? | **Barely, and never on Sunday.** `grep '\.age' lib/core/sim/game.ts` returns nothing. Age only reaches the field through offseason attribute drift, and `ageCurve()`'s decline is so shallow that after rounding it's often zero. There is no age-linked injury risk, fatigue, or recovery. |
| Do injuries matter? | **Almost not at all.** ~430 player-weeks missed league-wide per season (~13 per team; the NFL is an order of magnitude higher). Injuries are rolled and healed in the same tick, so every injury costs one fewer game than it says — the most common entry (bruised ribs, 1 week, weight 30/100) costs **zero** games. Nothing carries into the next season. No injuries occur in the playoffs at all. |
| Scouting? | Economy is real but the budget is a rounding error: 100 points ÷ 10/click = **2 prospects fully scouted out of a ~230-man class**. And the player page prints raw attributes for prospects, from which true OVR is exactly recoverable. |
| Drafting? | **The draft order is wrong every single year in every single save.** See P0-1. |
| Trades? | **There is no trade system.** `grep -ri trade` across the whole codebase returns one comment. `DraftPick.originalTeamId` exists and is never used. |
| Free agency? | Functional, but the user has **zero competition**. CPU waves only fire when the user presses a button, and one "Auto-fix" click signs the best available free agents at the league minimum. |
| Names / ages / background? | Names: 90 first × 100 last. 170 duplicate names among 2,435 living players by year 20. Background: **none** — no college, hometown, jersey number, height, weight, draft narrative, or personality. |

## P0 — fix before anything else

### P0-1. Draft order is computed from the wrong season and degenerates to team ID
`lib/core/offseason/draft.ts:105` — `draftOrder(state, season - 1)`. During the draft
`state.season` is the season just played; `season - 1`'s games were wiped at the previous
rollover (`offseason/index.ts:162  state.games = []`). So `computeRecords` returns 32 blank
records, every tiebreak falls through to `standings.ts:77  return a.teamId - b.teamId`, and
the order is constant.

Measured round-1 order, twenty consecutive seasons: `31,30,29,28,27,26,25,24…` — identical
every year, while the actual worst team finished 2-15.

**Consequence: a permanent, seed-independent competitive-balance failure.**

| | team 0 avg wins / 12 seasons | team 31 avg wins |
|---|---|---|
| seed 1 | 2.9 | 10.1 |
| seed 2 | 3.6 | 7.9 |
| seed 3 | 2.6 | 8.7 |

Team 31 gets the first pick forever; team 0 gets the last pick forever and finished worst in
the league in 12 of the last 13 seasons of the 20-year run. There is no tanking, no
worst-picks-first, no reason to care about your record once you're eliminated.

Also: `standings.ts:143`'s champion/runner-up lookup is off by one for the same reason —
*last* year's finalists get pushed to the back of the order.

Fix: `draftOrder(state, season)` and stop clearing `state.games` before the draft (or make
`computeRecords` fall back to `state.history[].standings`, which already holds the data).

### P0-2. "Auto-fill roster" signs anyone at the league minimum
`reconcileRoster` → `fillRoster` (`contracts.ts:206-213`) assigns free agents directly rather
than going through `signPlayer`, so `askingPrice` and the 92% floor never apply:

```ts
const pool = available().sort((a, b) => b.ovr - a.ovr);
pick.teamId = teamId;
pick.contract = makeContract(rng, LEAGUE_MINIMUM, 1, state.season, 0);
```

Measured: an 86 OVR TE asking $18.8M/yr signed for $895K. Because contracts expire before
free agency, the user *enters* the FA phase at 26/53 and the hub shows the "Auto-fix" card
unprompted — one click fills 27 slots with the 27 best free agents at minimum salary. The
entire free-agency screen is optional.

Related: `contracts.ts:302` silently rewrites the user's own expensive contracts down to the
league minimum as a last-resort cap fix, with no confirmation. In the 15-season probe one
team's payroll sat at exactly **$73M against a $577M cap** for ten straight seasons — a full
roster of minimum deals. The cap has stopped being a constraint for that franchise.

### P0-3. Defenders, kickers and punters never accumulate a single snap
`snaps++` appears in exactly three places in `sim/game.ts` (`:653`, `:731`, `:823`), all
offensive. Measured season averages: OL 552 snaps, QB 230, RB 116, WR 61 — **EDGE / DT / LB /
CB / S / K / P: 0.0.**

Three downstream failures:
1. **Fatigue is dead for every defender.** `att()` reads snap count; `sta` does nothing on defense.
2. **Progression is systematically skewed.** `progression.ts:39` — `snapShare = line.snaps / 700`
   feeds a +50% development bonus. Linemen get the full bonus, QBs 97% of it, **every defensive
   player gets zero.** This compounds every offseason and is the largest multi-season drift source.
3. Combined with `sim/game.ts:1264` (box-score lines are only written for players with a nonzero
   counting stat), **33% of the league finishes each season with fewer than 9 games played** —
   a starting corner who wasn't targeted simply didn't play, as far as the record book knows.

## P1 — the franchise arc doesn't work

### P1-1. Nothing gets old
`ageCurve()` (`progression.ts:18-28`) returns −0.28 for 1-2 years past peak and −0.75 for 3-4.
After `Math.round(delta)` most declines land on zero, and `awr/dec/dsc` are explicitly floored
so mental attributes *never* decline. The physical-decay pass (`:74-78`) rounds to 0 until a
player is 3+ years past peak.

Result — mean OVR by age, 2037 in the 15-season probe: **22 → 64.6, 26 → 67.4, 30 → 69.4,
34 → 71.8.** Survivorship explains some of it, but the direction should not be monotonic. A
34-year-old is never a declining asset, so "when do I move on from this guy" is never a decision.

Suggested: steepen post-peak decline ~2×, let the decline be *fractional* (accumulate a float
per attribute rather than rounding to an int each year), allow mental attributes to decline
after ~3 years past peak, and add an age term to injury risk and recovery time.

### P1-2. OVR inflates across the save
20-season run: mean active OVR 63.6 → 68.5 (plateau ~68.4), 85+ population 22 → 69, 90+ 4 → 17.
A "78 OVR starter" means something different in year 1 and year 15, which quietly breaks the
market curve (P1-3), the record book, and any tier language in the UI.

Cause is the interaction of P0-3 (uneven dev bonus), P1-1 (no decline), and a draft class whose
talent curve (`draft.ts:31-36`) doesn't scale with the league it's feeding.

### P1-3. The contract market has no ceiling
`marketApy` is `cap × 0.135 × ((ovr−55)/40)^2.4 × posMult × ageMult`, with `posMult(QB) = 3.4`:

| OVR | QB APY | % of cap |
|---|---|---|
| 85 | $58.7M | 23.0% |
| 90 | $85.0M | 33.3% |
| 95 | $117.0M | 45.9% |
| 99 | $147.2M | 57.7% |

The comment says "tuned so an elite QB lands near 20% of the cap" — true at 85, not above.
Highest single cap hit observed: **$207M against a $431M cap.** As OVR inflates (P1-2) these
appear more often. Needs a soft ceiling (asymptote toward ~25-28% of cap for a QB).

### P1-4. Injuries
- `engine.ts:116-117` — `rollInjuries(); healWeek();` in the same call. Every injury is one
  week shorter than reported; the 30%-weight 1-week injury costs nothing. Removes ~41% of all
  intended missed time.
- `rollInjuries` is flat ~1.2%/week on **every rostered player**, ignoring position, snap count,
  and whether the team even played (bye weeks included). A punter and a workhorse back carry
  identical risk.
- `playoffs.ts` calls neither `rollInjuries` nor `healWeek` — nobody gets hurt or heals in the
  entire postseason.
- `progression.ts:100` zeroes `injuryWeeks` every offseason, so a week-17 torn ACL (20-40 weeks)
  is fully healed by the opener. No lingering effects, no durability hit, no career-enders.
- No in-game injuries at all; `simulateGame` never writes `injuryWeeks`.

### P1-5. Free agency has no opponent
`runCpuFaRound` explicitly removes the user from the actor list (`freeAgency.ts:60-61`) and only
runs when the user clicks. The user browses a static 562-player pool with zero risk of losing a
target. `signPlayer` is a single binary threshold (`offer >= asking × 0.92`) with no counteroffer,
no player preference for winners/playing time/scheme, no repeated-attempt penalty. The UI prefills
100% of asking, so anyone using the prefill overpays by 8% versus the dominant strategy.

`GameState.fa`, `FaState` and `FaBid` are declared in `types.ts` and are **always null** — the
bidding system they describe was never built. The "Market Wave 1/4 — prices soften each wave"
copy is cosmetic; the wave counter is React-local state that resets on navigation and is unbounded.

### P1-6. No trades
Nothing. No pick trading, no player trades, no deadline, no trade value chart — even though
`POSITION_VALUE` and `originalTeamId` exist to support one. This is the largest missing feature
in the game and the one most players will notice first.

## P2 — scouting, draft and identity

- **Scouting budget is 2 prospects/year.** `SCOUT_COST = 10`, 100 points/season, `scoutProspect(+22)`
  per click, prospects start at 12. Four clicks takes one prospect from a ±11 band to ±2. Spread
  across the class instead and you get 10 players at ±9, which is statistically worthless against
  a ~230-man class.
- **Prospect true OVR is one click away.** `app/player/[id]/page.tsx:407` renders raw attributes with
  no `prospect` guard; `computeOvr` is a published fixed-weight sum of exactly those attributes.
- **The user's scouting improves CPU boards.** `scoutedOvrLow/High` live on the shared `Player`, and
  `cpuBoardValue` reads that band. Scouting a prospect makes every CPU team's read of him better too.
  CPU teams never spend scouting points of their own.
- **`draft/page.tsx:633`** — "Recent picks" calls `displayedOvr` on players whose `prospect` flag was
  just flipped to false, so it prints the *true* OVR of everyone the CPU has taken.
- **Rookie contracts aren't slotted.** `rookieContract` is flat per round — pick 1 and pick 32 sign the
  same deal, ~$4.1M APY for a first-rounder against a $255M cap.
- **`makePick` never checks `ROSTER_LIMIT`** for the user; `finalizeOffseason` then silently cuts the
  lowest-OVR surplus, plausibly the rookies just drafted.
- **Rookie of the Year is not restricted to rookies.** `progression.ts` gates on `p.yearsPro > 1`, so
  second-year players qualify. Confirmed: 2027 ROY had already played a full 2026 season. Generated
  22-year-olds on the initial rosters (`yearsPro = age − 22 = 0`) also win it.
- **Prospects don't age.** `runProgression` skips `prospect: true`, so a class generated one offseason
  early is drafted at the age it was born with.
- **Player identity is thin.** 90 first names × 100 last (× a suffix) → 170 shared names among 2,435
  living players by season 20, some three-deep. No college, hometown, height/weight, jersey number,
  combine numbers, or personality/trait. Coaches are a first name from a 20-item list plus the player
  surname list, and coaches never change, age, get fired, or have schemes.

## P3 — sim-engine realism (details in the engine audit)

Ranked highlights, all verified against a full instrumented season:

1. **No backup ever plays.** `ROTATION.QB = 1`, and `onField()` — the only substitution path — is
   called in exactly one place. Garbage-time substitution is dead code. QB2 threw 11.9 attempts per
   team-season, entirely from between-week injuries; QB3 threw zero.
2. **7 eligible receivers on every snap**, and the target/red-zone bonuses key on *array index*, so
   TE1 sits at index 4 with `roleBonus = 1` and `rzBonus = 0` — the tight end is structurally excluded
   from the red zone. RB2/RB3 caught 0.2 and 0.0 passes per team-season. League receptions leader: 156
   (NFL record 149).
3. **Stat leaders run hot every year.** Passing leader 5,346–6,445 across 20 seasons against an NFL
   record of 5,477 — the all-time mark is broken most years. Rushing leader hit 2,520 once.
4. **No clock management whatsoever** — no timeouts, two-minute drill, hurry-up, spikes, or kneels.
   Comeback drives are structurally near-impossible; aggregate pace only looks right because TOP is
   renormalized at the end.
5. **Overtime has no coin toss** — whoever was on defense when regulation expired automatically
   receives, in a sudden-death format. Large systematic bias.
6. Pick-six INT return yards double-counted; INT/fumble return yardage never moves the ball; rushing
   yards on a lost fumble are credited to nobody; `fourthDownAtt` double-counted by pre-snap penalties
   (46.8% measured vs 51.4% corrected); a sack can never produce a safety.
7. No dedicated returner — return duty is re-rolled per kick across 11 players, so no return specialist
   career can exist.

## P4 — save health

Measured `JSON.stringify` size, seed 7:

| season | peak in-season | after rollover | players | retired | log entries |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12.6 MB | 2.4 MB | 2,051 | 128 | 806 |
| 8 | 19.1 MB | 8.8 MB | 3,660 | 1,243 | ~7,900 |
| 20 | — | 19.8 MB | 6,658 | 4,223 | 17,514 |

- Current-season box scores are a flat ~10.5 MB and are ~80% of a year-1 save.
- `state.log` is never trimmed. Retired players are never pruned — 4,223 fully-hydrated corpses with
  attributes, every season line, and awards by year 20.
- `saveGame` does a full `JSON.parse(JSON.stringify(...))` deep clone of a 12-19 MB object on **every
  mutation** (`store/game.ts:110`) — every scout click, every depth-chart arrow — un-debounced.
- `listSaves()` returns full `GameState[]`; three franchises ≈ 50 MB of live objects to draw three rows.
  `SaveSummary` exists for exactly this and is never used.

## Simulations worth adding to the harness

`verify.ts` asserts *legality* (272 games, 53-man rosters, cap ≥ 0) and passes. None of the failures
above are legality failures — they're **calibration and coherence** failures, which is the gap.

Add these as failing assertions:

1. **Parity guard** — over N seasons and M seeds, no team's mean win total may differ from 8.5 by more
   than ~2. Catches P0-1 immediately.
2. **Draft-order guard** — the #1 pick belongs to a team in the bottom 3 of last season's standings.
3. **Ratings-drift guard** — league mean OVR and the 85+/90+ population must stay within a band across
   20 seasons.
4. **Age-curve guard** — mean OVR at 34 must be below mean OVR at 27.
5. **Snap-coverage guard** — every position group must average > 0 snaps; every rostered healthy
   starter must be credited ≥ 15 games.
6. **Record-book guard** — the single-season passing/rushing/receiving leader must exceed the real NFL
   record no more than ~1 season in 15.
7. **Injury-load guard** — league player-weeks missed per season must land in a target band (roughly
   10× current).
8. **Cap-realism guard** — no single cap hit above ~28% of the cap; no team payroll below ~75% of cap
   at season start.
9. **Save-growth guard** — post-rollover save size grows < X MB/season; `state.players.length` stays
   bounded.

`sweep.ts` at 40 random leagues × 2 seasons is too short to see any of this — the drift only becomes
visible around season 8-10. A 20-season × 5-seed nightly run would have caught every P0 and P1 here.
